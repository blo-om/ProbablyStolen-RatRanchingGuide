Before the threshold age, your rat has a 0% chance of dying from old age. Once it reaches that day, it must survive a flat 75% death roll every night:

02 Step 02: Hunger, Appetite & Feeding

Step 02 · 500 Max Reservoir
How Appetite Drains Meter 0–500
HungerToday = max(0, HungerYesterday - HungerStat) FoodRejected = [FoodMaxValue × 0.75 ≤ HungerToday]
Standard Daily Drain 65.0 Calories
Starting Newborn Stomach 500 (Species Default)
Starvation Trigger Hits 0 (Loses weight, can't mate)
Picky Eaters: Rats won't touch a food item if their current hunger already covers 75% of that item's value (FoodMaxValue × 0.75 ≤ HungerToday). This is relative to each food's own value, not a single fixed hunger number — a small snack still gets eaten well past the point a big meal would be rejected.

Neither InitAnimalItem nor GiveBirth sets a rat's Hunger tag explicitly at spawn, so newborns simply inherit AnimalSpecies's base-stats default of 500 — the full reservoir, not a smaller starting stomach.

Penalties for Underfed Rats Thresholds: 398 & 200
Stunted Growth (Step 08) < 398 Calories (-1% to -50% weight gain)
Lowered Fertility (Step 07) < 200 Calories (Up to -40% mating odds)
Breeding Goal: Breeding Hunger Rate down allows rats to stay above the 398 growth threshold for days on a single feeding.

HandlePregnancyProgress confirms a real miscarriage mechanic: every night while pregnant, if a specific status flag is active, there's a flat 75% chance to lose the pregnancy instead of progressing toward birth (otherwise progress just ticks up by 1, and birth fires once it reaches the species' pregnancy duration).

Instant Kill: Overcrowding is not a damage tick—it's an instant kill. The game always picks the lightest living rat that weighs less than the rat rolling the check. Runts and newborns die first.
Roommate Turn Order Step 06 Timing

Runs at Step 06 (after feeding and health updates, but before fertility, growth, and mating). Any rat killed by crowding gets no weight gain and cannot mate.

Chain Reaction: Rats check overcrowding one by one. If an earlier check kills a runt, the pen clears up slightly, which can spare the next rat in line.

Harem Sizing: CheckMate shuffles the eligible, non-pregnant, opposite-sex roommates and walks that list once, trying a different partner on each of the (up to) 3 attempts — not 3 rolls against the same rat. With fewer than 3 eligible partners available, the extra attempts just go unused that night. Keeping a few spare females in the pen ensures a male's full nightly budget gets used, since a female becomes ineligible the moment she's pregnant.

11 Why Breeding Hits a Wall Around 2.5× to 2.6×

Colony Dynamics
In Pure Code: No True Ceiling Step Size Stays Constant

Even at extreme stats, the absolute jump size never shrinks to zero:

AbsoluteStep = Value × Magnitude → 0.04 × Baseline (Regular) AbsolutePullback = 0.25 × AbsoluteStep → 0.01 × Baseline (Regular) NetMaxStep (Regular) = +0.03 × Baseline NetMaxStep (Wide) = +0.09 × Baseline (3× Step, 3× Pullback)

Because the step size never vanishes, strict elite pairs can climb indefinitely in code.

In Practice: The Casual Plateau Stalls Around ~2.4 – 2.6x
Positive Mutation Chance 5.625% per allele birth
Negative Drag Chance 9.375% (1.67× more frequent)
Wide Positive Mutation 0.28% per allele birth
Gain on a Single Hit +0.015 (Regular) / +0.045 (Wide)
The Dilution Trap: Pairing an elite 2.60 rat with a 2.40 rat resets the offspring's starting baseline to 2.50. Making up that 0.10 gap requires multiple rare positive mutations before old age claims the parents. In mixed pens, progress naturally grinds to a halt around 2.4–2.6×.
