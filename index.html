Longevity isn't an execution date—it's an alarm clock for when dangerous nightly dice rolls begin:

Longevity 1.00 (Standard) Rolls start on Day 14
Longevity 1.50 (Selective) Rolls start on Day 21
State & Threshold
a ∈ ℕ₀ (age, days) glong ∈ ℝ⁺ (Longevity trait) α ∈ {0,1} (alive/dead) Acrit = 14 × glong χsenescent = [a ≥ Acrit] P(Mortality | a) = 0.75 × χsenescent α′ = α × (1 − Bernoulli(P(Mortality)))
Multi-Night Survival
P(survive k more nights | senescent) = (1 − 0.75)k = 0.25k = 4−k limk→∞ P(survive) = 0 P(k=1) = 25.00% P(k=2) = 6.25% P(k=4) ≈ 0.39%

Every night past Acrit is an independent Bernoulli trial — survival probability decays geometrically, never resetting and never reaching a guaranteed-death floor. The "14" lives as a 6th entry in the same per-species array that holds the 5 growth-stage boundaries (growthStages[5]), not a separate hardcoded literal — the master nightly dispatcher reads that exact array slot, multiplies it by Longevity, and compares against age before rolling the flat 75%.

Survival Past the Threshold 75% Death Roll / Night

Before the threshold age, your rat has a 0% chance of dying from old age. Once it reaches that day, it must survive a flat 75% death roll every night:

Before the Threshold 100% Safe from Old Age
First Night Past Threshold 25.00% Chance to Live
Second Night Past Threshold 6.25% Chance to Live
Third Night Past Threshold 1.56% Chance to Live
Fourth Night Past Threshold 0.39% Chance to Live
Rancher Takeaway: Because every night is an independent coin flip, lucky rats can occasionally live for days past their threshold (e.g. out to Day 26). But practically, don't count on breeders surviving more than 48 hours once their clock rings.

Picky Eaters: Rats won't touch a food item if their current hunger already covers 75% of that item's value (FoodMaxValue × 0.75 ≤ HungerToday) — relative to each food's own value, not a single fixed hunger number like "425." What starving actually costs you: hitting 0 Hunger doesn't kill on the spot — it just sets a starving flag. Death only follows if that flag is still set the next night (a full day/night cycle at 0 with nothing eaten); Hunger rising above 0 clears the flag immediately. Short of that, a starving rat still pays every night: weight change flips negative at half the magnitude of its normal rate, guaranteed loss regardless of stage (Step 08), and a pregnant starving rat rolls a flat 75% chance to lose the pregnancy instead of progressing (Step 09) — both gated by the exact same starving flag.

Myth, likely busted: a popular claim holds that sick rats raise the disease odds for healthy cage-mates, and that high-Immunity rats speed up sick neighbors' recovery. The Partner Bottleneck Mating Gating

Step 07 locks in each rat's effective fertility for tonight's mating in Step 09.

The Weak Link Rule: Pregnancy chance uses min(Fertility_A, Fertility_B), not the average. If your stud male has 90% fertility but is paired with a sick 15% female, your odds are stuck at 15%. Keep both partners healthy!
Myth, busted: a long-standing community belief holds that pregnancy chance is the average of both partners' fertility. Elder Shrinkage: Elders lose about 17g every night, and that loss scales with Growth Rate. Fast-growing rats shrink fastest in old age. Harvest on Day 10 before shrinkage starts — there is no separate "entry penalty," the first shrinking night is simply the night the rat turns 11.
Longevity does not delay shrinkage: which stage rate applies is decided by age alone up through Day 13, so Elder shrinkage begins on Day 11 for every rat. Longevity only matters from Day 14 on: at Longevity ≤ 1 the weight simply freezes there, while at Longevity > 1 the rat keeps shrinking at the Elder rate until it reaches Day 14 × Longevity (the same point where the 75% nightly death roll starts), then freezes.
Weight floor: a rat can never drop below 25% of its current stage's checkpoint weight (about 12g as a juvenile, 50g as an adolescent, 88g as an adult, 112g as an elder, 100g past Day 14), so starvation and shrinkage can't take it to nothing.

Harem Sizing: CheckMate shuffles the eligible, non-pregnant, opposite-sex roommates and walks that list once, trying a different partner on each of the (up to) 3 attempts — not 3 rolls against the same rat. With fewer than 3 eligible partners available, the extra attempts just go unused that night. Attempts are also mutual, not one-sided: the targeted female spends one of her own nightly attempts just by being on the receiving end, whether she initiated anything or not, and every rat's counter resets to 0 at the start of each night before mating runs — it's strictly a per-night budget. Keeping a few spare females in the pen ensures a male's full nightly budget gets used, since a female becomes ineligible the moment she's pregnant.

Wide Jackpot Roll 5% of mutations roll wide (0.75% per allele/birth overall)
Max Step Size 0.04 × Baseline (at high drift)
Max Pullback Drag 0.01 × Baseline (at high drift)
In Plain English: Every allele gets two coin flips: a 15% chance to mutate at all, then (only if that hits) a 5% chance to triple the jump size — so a true jackpot only happens on 0.75% of all rolls, not 5%. The jump itself starts at 8% of the current value, but it shrinks the further that value already sits from the species baseline: an allele parked right at baseline can swing close to the full 8%, one already twice baseline might only manage 2–3%. Every mutation also carries a small automatic nudge back toward baseline — about a quarter the size of the jump — that always points home, never away. Worked example on Growth Rate (baseline 1.0): an allele sitting at 1.0 can land anywhere from 0.92 to 1.08 on a hit; one already parked at 2.0 can only move between about 1.94 and 2.04, because both the smaller jump and the constant homeward nudge are working against it.
Built-in Forward Bias: The forward jump (+1.0×Mag) beats the maximum backward pull (-0.25×Mag). About 37.5% of successful mutations produce net positive drift.

11 Why Breeding Hits a Wall Around 2.5× to 2.6×

Colony Dynamics
In Pure Code: No True Ceiling
Step Size Stays Constant

Even at extreme stats, the absolute jump size never shrinks to zero:

AbsoluteStep = Value × Magnitude → 0.04 × Baseline (Regular) AbsolutePullback = 0.25 × AbsoluteStep → 0.01 × Baseline (Regular) NetMaxStep (Regular) = +0.03 × Baseline NetMaxStep (Wide) = +0.09 × Baseline (3× Step, 3× Pullback)

Because the step size never vanishes, strict elite pairs can climb indefinitely in code.

11.1 — Neutral Attractor & Global Stability
E[ΔV | V] = 0.0066 × V(B − V) ÷ [B(2D+1)2] V > B ⇒ E[ΔV] < 0 // pulled back down V < B ⇒ E[ΔV] > 0 // pulled back up V = B ⇒ E[ΔV] ≡ 0 // fixed point ∂⁄∂V · E[ΔV] |V=B = −0.0066 < 0 // negative Jacobian: V*=B is locally (and, by the sign check above, globally) stable

Derived by taking the expectation of 𝑀(V,B)'s update over Mroll, W, and u (which drops out since E[u]=0), then simplifying D·sgn(B−V) to (B−V)÷B. Baseline isn't just "where mutations happen to cluster" — it's a provably attracting equilibrium in expectation, which is exactly why species stats don't drift away from their base value on their own.

11.2 — Upward Invariant Velocity
limD→∞ (V × M(D,W)) = 0.04 × B × (1+2W) limD→∞ (V × Pullback) = −0.01 × B × (1+2W) ΔV∞ = B(1+2W) × (0.04u − 0.01), u ∼ Uniform(−1,1) Regular (W=0): ΔV ∈ [−0.05B, +0.03B] Wide (W=1): ΔV ∈ [−0.15B, +0.09B] ΔV∞ > 0 ⇔ u > 0.25 P(net positive | Mroll=1) = (1−0.25)÷2 = 37.5% // invariant - doesn't depend on how far V has drifted P(net negative | Mroll=1) = 62.5% (1.67× more frequent)

The 37.5% figure is the asymptotic (D→∞) frequency — the same derivation in 11.1 shows the split is a plain 50/50 near Baseline, where Pullback is negligible. It only sharpens to 37.5/62.5 once a stat has drifted far out, and stays there rather than sharpening further.

11.3 — Downward Behavior Near the Floor
// as V falls toward 0.01, D=(B-V)/B stays BOUNDED (-> 1), unlike D -> infinity going up: AbsoluteStep = V × M(D,W) → 0 as V → 0 // because V itself vanishes, not because M(D,W) does // Pullback's sign flips for V Pullback(D,W,V) > 0 for all V < B // D changes slowly with V near the floor, so each roll multiplies V by a near-constant factor: Vn+1 ≈ Vn × (1 + Mroll·(u·M(D,W) + Pullback)) // -> compounding negative rolls shrink V geometrically

Careful reading required here: this is not a second attractor — 11.1 already proved V*=B is the only stable fixed point, and Pullback is strictly positive below Baseline, actively resisting the slide. The 0.01 floor is a hard max(0.01, ...) clamp, only reached if enough negative rolls compound faster than Pullback corrects for it. Absolute step size shrinks near the floor simply because the step scales with V itself, which is already small there — not because the underlying Magnitude term is shrinking (it stays roughly bounded, unlike the D→∞ regime in 11.2).

11.4 — Stochastic Soft Ceiling: Mutation vs. Dilution
P(positive) = 0.15 × 0.375 = 5.625% P(negative) = 0.15 × 0.625 = 9.375% P(wide ∧ positive) = 0.15×0.05×0.375 = 0.28125% P(both alleles +, same birth) = (0.05625)2 ≈ 0.316% // pairing an elite rat (V=2.6) with a sub-elite mate (V=2.4): ΔVdilution = (2.4 − 2.6) ÷ 2 = −0.10 phenotype // vs. the max a single regular hit can repay: ΔVsingle-hit = +0.03 allele ⇒ +0.015 phenotype

Because ΔV∞ never shrinks to zero (11.2), there is no code-level ceiling — the step size at extreme deviation stays a constant fraction of Baseline forever. The ~2.4–2.6× wall is what happens when a −0.10 dilution hit meets a single mutation's max +0.015 phenotype gain: dilution wins more often than not (needing the 0.316% dual-allele-positive case, or several generations, to catch up), but a colony that only ever breeds within its current elite tier sidesteps the dilution term entirely and is not mathematically blocked from climbing further — just much slower.

In Practice: The Casual Plateau Stalls Around ~2.4 – 2.6x
Positive Mutation Chance 5.625% per allele birth
Negative Drag Chance 9.375% (1.67× more frequent)
Wide Positive Mutation 0.28% per allele birth
Gain on a Single Hit +0.015 (Regular) / +0.045 (Wide)
The Dilution Trap: Pairing an elite 2.60 rat with a 2.40 rat resets the offspring's starting baseline to 2.50. Making up that 0.10 gap requires multiple rare positive mutations before old age claims the parents. In mixed pens, progress naturally grinds to a halt around 2.4–2.6×.

12 Prion Disease (Why You Never Feed Them Rat Meat)

Colony Collapse Hazard
Contamination & Permanent Penalty
CheckCannibalism
RequiresCannibalism = [Food's species-of-origin == Eater's own species] IsFoodFlagged = [Food already carries Prion flag] InfectionChance = RequiresCannibalism × (1.0 - (1.0 - IsFoodFlagged) × (Immunity ÷ 100)) On Infection: Permanent -20% penalty applied simultaneously to Growth, Immunity, and Longevity.
It's cannibalism specifically: CheckCannibalism only ever fires when the meat's species-of-origin matches the eater's own species — feed a rat any other species' meat and this entire mechanic never triggers, Prion flag or not. Only rat-on-rat feeding is at risk. Within that: meat already carrying the Prion flag infects 100% of the time, ignoring Immunity completely; clean rat meat instead rolls 1 - Immunity/100.
Vicious Cycle: Butchering an infected rat permanently tags all resulting meat cuts. Feeding that meat back to your pen infects every rat that eats it — each one takes the permanent −20% on Growth, Immunity and Longevity, though the damage stays with the rat and isn't passed to its pups.
Transmission & Permanent Degradation
[Cannibalism] = [FoodSpecies == EaterSpecies] // CheckCannibalism's very first gate; anything else returns immediately, no roll at all P(Infection) = [Cannibalism] × (1.0 − (1.0 − [FoodPrion]) × (gimm ÷ 100.0)) [FoodPrion]=1 ⇒ P(Infection) ≡ 1.00 // bypasses Immunity entirely [FoodPrion]=0 ⇒ P(Infection) = 1 − gimm÷100.0 // clean raw meat, same species // on infection, permanent multiplicative penalty: bonus%k ← bonus%k − 0.20 ∀k ∈ {growth, imm, long} // effective stat = (1 + bonus%) × allele mean; alleles untouched [CarcassPrion] = [RatInfected] // flag propagates into every butchered cut

The -20% penalty uses the exact same coefficient as Section 04's spontaneous-sickness formula, applied here to Growth, Immunity, and Longevity simultaneously rather than gating a sickness roll. The carcass-flag propagation into every cut is handled directly in OnButcher. The penalty is stored as a modifier on the infected rat (ModBonusPercent adds −0.2 to each stat's _BONUS_PERCENT tag, and the game reads every stat as (1 + bonus%) × allele mean), so its alleles are never touched. It is permanent for that rat, but its pups inherit the original, clean alleles — the damage does not pass down the bloodline.

13 How To Value Each Stat

Breeding Priorities
Which Stats Actually Move The Needle
Grounded In Verified Formulas

Not every point of stat growth is worth the same. Some stats scale linearly forever, some hit a hard wall, and one only protects value instead of adding it. Here's what each of the seven mutable traits is actually doing, pulled straight from the formulas above — not a guess.

Growth Rate Linear & uncapped — also worsens Elder shrinkage if unharvested past Day 10
Litter Size Linear & uncapped, and it compounds every generation (female-expressed only)
Immunity Strong value up to 50, then hard-diminishing returns past it
Fertility Linear until a hard cap at 100 — gated by whichever partner is lower
Longevity Delays the death-roll clock; does nothing for Day 11 Elder shrinkage
Hunger Rate Adds no value directly — protects Growth/Fertility from decaying
Max Health Doesn't affect weight gain at all — only buys survival buffer
Bottom line: Litter Size and Growth Rate are the two that keep paying off no matter how far you push them — there's no cap in the formula. Immunity and Fertility are both "hit the number and stop" investments (50 and 100 respectively); anything spent past that is wasted breeding effort. Longevity is a niche pick that only pays off if you deliberately keep breeders alive past Day 10–14 for extra litters. Hunger Rate never shows up in a payout formula itself — it just keeps your rats above the 400/200-calorie thresholds longer per feeding, which is what actually protects your Growth and Fertility numbers. Max Health is the weakest pick of all seven: it never touches weight, fertility, or growth — the only thing it buys is more nights of buffer against the flat 25%-of-Max-Health disease tick and the sub-25-Immunity bleed.

Hunger Rate has no entry here because it never appears on the right-hand side of a payout formula — it only appears in Hdrain = max(0, H − ghunger), which determines how long you stay above the 400/200 thresholds that gate Growth and Fertility. Its value is entirely borrowed from whichever of those two stats it's currently protecting. Max Health has no entry either: it never appears in the Growth, Fertility, or Litter formulas at all — its only appearance anywhere is as the multiplier base in the Regen/SicknessDamage terms above (Step 05). Reducing infection odds is Immunity's job, not Health's.
